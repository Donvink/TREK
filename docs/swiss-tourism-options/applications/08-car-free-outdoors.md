## Innovation (confidential)

字符数：875｜限制：500–1500（含空格）

```text
An outdoor route and its public-transport access are often planned separately, especially when the walk ends somewhere different from its start. Our innovation is joint feasibility: verify outward and return connections, include access walks, allow for rest and buffers, and explain shorter exits. Missing route or condition data is treated as unknown rather than evidence of safety. We will benchmark against SwitzerlandMobility, maps, SBB and users' manual planning. The intended difference is a complete, limited-scope outing whose transport and route assumptions are traceable. Our lead would come from lawful route content, maintained connections, field checks and accommodation or community distribution. TREK's existing road routing is not a validated outdoor engine, and AGPL obligations remain. We do not claim a patent, universal route safety or guaranteed returns.
```

## Technology & Product (confidential)

字符数：1134｜限制：500–1500（含空格）

```text
This is a design-stage proposal, not an operational hiking service. We would reuse TREK maps, GPX display, itinerary, weather, transit and offline capabilities, then add verified route segments, exit nodes and a connection-feasibility module. GeoAdmin data and transit services are candidates whose terms, coverage and limits require testing. The first release would contain 10-15 low-intensity, daylight routes in one region. Six-week work covers interviews, lawful content sourcing, field checks, route/transport matching, mobile saved plans and actual trials. Later expansion could add seasonal packages or hotel distribution. Risks include missing closures, inaccurate duration, licensing and stale return data. We will test access walks, transport calendars, buffers, exits and snapshot age. The product will not evaluate mountaineering, avalanche or other safety-critical conditions. We plan to add an OJP 2.0 adapter to TREK's existing transit module, mapping Swiss journey queries to its common itinerary format for connection and return-time checks. API keys stay server-side; feed access and coverage remain to be validated.
```
