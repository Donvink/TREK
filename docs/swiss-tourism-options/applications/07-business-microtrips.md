## Innovation (confidential)

字符数：908｜限制：500–1500（含空格）

```text
Business and conference travelers may have a useful gap in their schedule but cannot easily judge what fits before the next commitment. Our innovation is deadline-first planning: work backward from a required arrival, reserve explicit buffers and match only experiences with sufficient usable time. Luggage needs, opening conditions and shortened routes are part of feasibility, not afterthoughts. We will compare this approach with event guides, maps, hotel advice and general itinerary tools. The planned advantage is reliable scope and transparent return assumptions rather than an assurance that no one will miss a flight. Our intended lead is reusable venue-area content and distribution through event organizers or business hotels. TREK-derived code follows AGPL; we do not claim patented routing or exclusive upstream capabilities. Channel relationships and verified execution must establish the lead.
```

## Technology & Product (confidential)

字符数：1141｜限制：500–1500（含空格）

```text
The concept is at design stage, with TREK's itinerary, maps, transit and calendar features as candidate foundations. We propose an event-profile module, availability windows, reverse-time feasibility and return rechecks. Initial scope is one venue area and 10-15 verified short routes; manual time input avoids access to full corporate calendars. Over six weeks we plan organizer interviews, local checks and manual recommendations, then mobile entry pages, time constraints and an event or hotel trial. Medium-term work could add opt-in reminders and companion itineraries if demand is verified. Risks include timetable gaps, delays, unknown luggage storage and timezone errors. The service will use the user's explicit arrival deadline, not certify airport processing time or guarantee flight connections. Tests cover shortened windows, return changes, session privacy and failed notifications. We plan to add an OJP 2.0 adapter to TREK's existing transit module, mapping Swiss journey queries to its common itinerary format for connection and return-time checks. API keys stay server-side; feed access and coverage remain to be validated.
```
